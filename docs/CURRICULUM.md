# CURRICULUM — training a domain expert, not a general model

A general model can see its corpus in random order, because its job is next-token statistics. A model meant
to **reason from first principles about traditional medicine and propose new methods** cannot: the data has a
dependency order, and training on shuffled everything teaches the model that 本草綱目 and a botany primer
are the same kind of thing.

So the corpus is served in stages, built from the registry's own metadata (`tradition`, `lang`, `tier`) —
not hand-picked files — which means the curriculum regenerates when the corpus grows.

```bash
python training/curriculum.py --build-only     # show the stages
python training/curriculum.py                  # train through them in order
```

## The stages, and why each comes where it does

| # | stage | what it selects | why it comes here | docs | chars |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **FOUNDATIONS** | `tradition: foundational` | physics, chemistry, biology, botany, anatomy — the first-principles substrate every later claim is checked against | 10,753 | 16.3 M |
| 1 | **ASIAN CLASSICS** | `tradition: tcm`, `tier 2`, 繁體/简体 | the primary canon in the original scripts, before any secondary retelling | 8,001 | 13.9 M |
| 2 | **REGIONAL TRADITIONS** | ayurveda · unani · korean · acupuncture | the neighbouring systems, so the model can compare rather than memorise one | 7,184 | 12.6 M |
| 3 | **ALCHEMY & PROCESS** | `tradition: alchemy` | the operations that make a preparation work — 炮製, distillation, calcination | 803 | 1.3 M |
| 4 | **PATTERNS** | structured tables rendered as text | compound → plant → use → property → incompatibility, as prose rather than a lookup table | 13,250 | 0.2 M |
| 5 | **PHARMACOLOGY & METHOD** | pharmacognosy / pharmacology / materia medica titles | how a discovery is actually made — the method, not more facts | 7,123 | 11.5 M |
| 6 | **GROUNDING** | (passages, question, answer) pairs, **including negatives** | citation discipline: answer over retrieved spans, say "not recorded" when the span is absent | 725 | 0.1 M |
| 7 | **SELF-OBSERVATION** | not data — a probe on hidden states | teach the model to read its own activations for groundedness | — | — |

The learning rate anneals **0.82× per stage**, so later stages refine rather than overwrite: the canon
should not be blown away by the pharmacology pass.

## Stage 6 is the one most projects skip

Grounding is a **two-sided** skill. A model trained only on examples where the answer *is* in the passage
learns to always answer — that is confabulation with extra steps. So the grounding corpus contains both
kinds:

```
POSITIVE
Passages: [1] … 甘草：性 平，味 甘，歸經 心/肺/脾/胃 …
Question: What is the 歸經 of 甘草?
Answer:   According to the record: 甘草：性 平，味 甘，歸經 心/肺/脾/胃。

NEGATIVE   ← 600 of the 725 pairs
Passages: [1] … 麻黃：性 溫，味 辛 …
Question: What is the 歸經 of 甘草?
Answer:   The corpus does not record this. The supplied passage is about 麻黃, not 甘草.
```

Generated by `scripts/build_grounding_pairs.py`. The negative ratio is deliberate and high.

## Stage 7 — self-observation, measured

The brief's last requirement is that the model **observe its own neuronal activations**: recognise when it is
answering from grounded knowledge and when it is confabulating. Prompting it to say "I am confident" is a
sentence, not a measurement. So we measure the representation directly:

1. build pairs — a passage that **does** contain the answer (label 1) versus one about a different herb (label 0);
2. take the pooled hidden state at the final token, per layer;
3. fit a linear probe per layer and report AUC on held-out pairs.

```bash
python training/self_observe.py --ckpt training/runs/<run>/ckpt-best.pt
```

**Result on the base MoE (19.3 M, step 199, before the curriculum):**

| layer | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| probe AUC | 0.348 | 0.508 | 0.446 | 0.502 | 0.496 | 0.506 | **0.521** |

**Best layer 0.521 — chance.** The model does **not** yet represent grounding in a linearly decodable way.

That is the honest baseline, and it is the number the curriculum has to move. Two readings are possible and
we do not yet know which:

- the model is too undertrained to have formed the feature (likely — it saw ~1.6 M tokens), or
- grounding is not linearly represented in a 6-layer d256 model regardless of training.

Either way, publishing 0.521 is more useful than publishing a nice-sounding claim. **A probe that decodes
grounding means the information is present; it does not mean the model uses it** — that gap is exactly what
stage 6 training is meant to close, and it is reported rather than assumed.

## Measured outcome — the honest result

The curriculum ran end to end: **7 stages, 473 steps, 3,874,816 tokens, ~80 min on CPU**.

| stage | 0 FOUND | 1 CANON | 2 REGIONAL | 3 ALCHEMY | 4 PATTERNS | 5 PHARM | 6 GROUND |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| val loss (stage's own held-out) | 6.832 | 6.794 | 2.705 | 6.651 | 6.404 | 6.827 | 6.227 |

Those per-stage numbers are computed on **each stage's own** held-out slice, so they are **not comparable
across stages** — a lower number at stage 2 means the regional corpus is easier, not that the model improved.

### Head-to-head on identical held-out data

| checkpoint | held-out CE | step | tokens seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| base MoE (`moe-r1`) | **6.9776** | 199 | 1,638,400 |
| curriculum (`stage 6`) | 6.9958 | 472 | 3,874,816 |

**The curriculum checkpoint is marginally *worse* (+0.018 CE) on general held-out text.** That is not a
refutation of curriculum training — it spent its last 3.9 M tokens on stage-ordered domain data at a low
learning rate — but it *is* a refutation of claiming a win at this budget, and it is published here rather
than quietly dropped.

### Self-observation did move, slightly

| | best layer | AUC |
|---|---|---|
| before (base, step 199) | 6 | 0.521 |
| after (curriculum, step 472) | 2 | **0.568** |

Direction is right, effect is weak at n = 240, and **behavioural grounding was 0/6** — on negative examples
the model never emitted "the corpus does not record this". So citation discipline did **not** transfer. The
probe got a little better at decoding the representation; the policy did not change.

### Why

**1.4 % of the needed tokens.** This run added 3.87 M; cumulative is 5.47 M. Compute-optimal at 19.3 M
parameters is roughly 20 × parameters ≈ **390 M tokens**. No curriculum, grounding corpus or probe
substitutes for tokens at this scale.

The infrastructure is ready and measured. The number that has to change is the token count, and that is a GPU
job (`make train-flagship`), not a CPU one.

## Honest status

- The staged trainer, the grounding-pair builder and the probe are implemented and run.
- The curriculum ran (473 steps, 3.87 M tokens) and **did not beat the base model** on general held-out data;
  the self-observation probe moved 0.521 → 0.568, which is direction without capability.
- No claim about the weights being a domain expert is made. The expertise that *is* demonstrable lives in
  retrieval, the structured chemistry, and the physics arbiter.
